OSlingo Support

Operating-systems puzzles for CS 326 at USF, one small problem per screen. Free, with no account.

What OSlingo is

OSlingo is a study app built for CS 326 (Operating Systems) at USF. It is not an official university app. It follows the course week by week. Each week has short lessons and puzzles you answer with taps: pick an answer, fix a line of Rust, split an address into its bit fields, fill in a scheduler's timeline, or put the steps of a system in order. Every puzzle explains its answer once you solve it. Skills brings back the concepts you have met for a quick review.

All weeks are open from the start. Within a week, decks open one after another.

OSlingo never shows an OSlings solution. Its puzzles sit beside the OSlings exercises. They do not replace them.

Install it

OSlingo runs on iPhone, on Android and in your browser. The phone app is a test build for the students of CS 326.

iPhone (iOS 16.4 or later)

  1. Install TestFlight from the App Store.
  2. On your iPhone, open the TestFlight public link. It is posted on Canvas or Zulip.
  3. Tap View in TestFlight (or Start Testing), then Accept, then Install.

TestFlight lists the app as CS 326 OSlingo. On your home screen it is OSlingo. A TestFlight build works for 90 days. TestFlight offers each newer build when it comes out.

Android (7.0 or later)

  1. Use a personal Google account, such as a Gmail account, not your USF account. University accounts may be blocked from testing programs.
  2. With that account, join the Google Group cs-326-oslingo-testers (tap Join group).
  3. Open the Google Play testing opt-in link while you are signed in to the same account, and tap Become a tester. The link is posted on Canvas or Zulip.
  4. From that page, follow the link to Google Play and install CS 326 OSlingo.

If Google Play says the app is not available, check that the Play Store uses the same personal account (tap your profile picture to switch). Joining the group can also take a few minutes to reach Google Play.

The web version

On the course site, click OSlingo in the top bar, or open cs326-f26.cs.usfca.edu/oslingo/. It runs in your browser and keeps your progress in that browser. Use Safari or Chrome, not another app's built-in browser.

Back up and move your progress

Your progress stays where you made it: on that phone, or in that browser. Nothing syncs on its own. A backup file is how you keep it and move it.

  • Back up: Settings › Progress › Back up. In the phone app, the share sheet opens. Save the file to your phone's files, or send it to yourself. On the web, the browser downloads the file.
  • Restore: on the other phone or browser, Settings › Progress › Restore from a file, and pick the file. It adds the file's progress to what is already there. Nothing there is lost, and restoring the same file again changes nothing.
  • Between the phone and the web: the same file works in both.
  • Start over: Settings › Progress › Delete progress on this device. Back up first if you may want your progress again.

OSlingo never sends your backup file anywhere. You choose where it goes.

Questions and answers

  • Does it need the internet? The phone app does not. Everything is inside the app, and it works in airplane mode. The web version needs the course site to load.
  • Do I need an account? No. There is nothing to sign in to.
  • Can the course staff see my progress? No. OSlingo sends nothing anywhere. The privacy policy has the details.

Report a problem

Found a bug, a puzzle that seems wrong, or a confusing explanation? Write to gdb.gena1@gmail.com. On iPhone, you can also take a screenshot in the app and send it as TestFlight feedback. It helps to include:

  • the puzzle's title and its week;
  • the app version (Settings, then About);
  • your phone or browser, and its version;
  • what you expected, and what happened instead.

Please do not send your own code for a course exercise. Bring exercise questions to office hours or the course forum.
