OSlingo Privacy Policy

Effective 6 November 2026

In short: OSlingo collects nothing. It has no account, no analytics, no ads and no crash reporting. The phone app makes no network requests. The web version loads only the course site's own files. Your progress stays on your phone or in your browser. TestFlight and Google Play, which deliver the test builds, collect their own data, as described below.

OSlingo is a free study app built for CS 326 (Operating Systems) at USF. It is not an official university app. It comes in two forms, and this policy covers both:

  • the phone app, for iPhone and Android. Students install a test build through Apple's TestFlight or through a Google Play test;
  • the web version, which runs in your browser on the course site at cs326-f26.cs.usfca.edu/oslingo/.

What OSlingo collects

Nothing. OSlingo does not collect, send, sell or share any personal data or usage data. There is no account and nothing to sign in to. There are no analytics, no ads, no tracking, no crash reporting and no third-party code that collects data. OSlingo does not use your location, contacts, camera, microphone or photos.

What is stored on your device or in your browser

To remember where you are, OSlingo saves a small amount of data on your phone or in your browser:

  • your progress: for each lesson and puzzle, whether you solved it, your best rank and result, how many attempts you made, how many checks and hints your first solve took, and when;
  • your review ratings on the cards in Skills, with the dates you gave them;
  • the resume point: the puzzle to pick up next;
  • your settings: the appearance, haptics, and the week you last picked on Home;
  • the first-run flag: whether you have seen the welcome.

None of it leaves your phone or your browser through OSlingo. It goes somewhere else only if you make a backup file and send that file yourself.

In the phone app, this data is in the app's own storage on your phone. Deleting the app deletes it. If your phone's own backup (iCloud or Google) is turned on, that backup may include it. Apple or Google handles that backup under its own policy, and OSlingo never sees it.

In the web version, this data is in your browser's storage for the course site, under names that start with oslingo:. Another browser, another device and a private window each keep their own. A private window loses it when it closes, and clearing the site's data in your browser deletes it. OSlingo asks the browser to keep this storage, and Settings shows whether it agreed. OSlingo sets no cookies.

Back up and Restore

Settings › Progress › Back up makes one small file with your progress, your review ratings and the resume point. The file also records when it was made and which OSlingo made it: the platform, the app version and the content build. Your settings are not in it.

  • On the web, the browser downloads the file, or hands it to the share sheet if you choose Share backup file.
  • In the phone app, OSlingo writes the file to its own temporary folder and opens the share sheet. You choose where the file goes, such as Files or a message to yourself. The copy in the temporary folder stays on the phone until the phone clears that folder or you delete the app.

You save or share the file. OSlingo never sends it anywhere and never learns where it went. The course staff see it only if you send it to them yourself.

Settings › Progress › Restore from a file reads a file you pick, on this device, and adds its progress to what is already there. It sends the file nowhere. The same file works in the phone app and on the web.

Delete progress on this device

Settings › Progress › Delete progress on this device removes your progress on lessons and puzzles, your review ratings and the resume point from this phone or this browser. Your settings stay. It does not touch a backup file you made or another device.

Network

The phone app makes no network requests. Every lesson, puzzle and answer check is built into the app, so it works the same in airplane mode. The Android app does not even ask for internet access. The app has one link, Privacy policy in About. Tapping it opens this page in your browser, outside OSlingo.

The web version loads from the course site. Every lesson and puzzle comes with the page. After the page loads, OSlingo fetches only the course site's own files, such as the code for another screen. It loads nothing from other sites. OSlingo never sends your answers or your progress anywhere.

GitHub, which hosts the course site

The course site, with the web version and this page, is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub keeps ordinary server logs of page requests. GitHub's documentation says that it logs and stores the IP address of every visitor to a GitHub Pages site, for security purposes. Its privacy statement also names the date and time of a visit and the pages viewed. GitHub handles these logs under its own privacy statement. The requests carry nothing about your progress or your answers.

TestFlight and Google Play

You install the phone app's test build through TestFlight or Google Play. Those are Apple's and Google's services. They collect their own data, under their own terms and policies, not under this one.

  • TestFlight. Apple's TestFlight terms say that Apple and the developer automatically collect crash logs and usage data from a test build. App Store Connect shows the developer each tester's install date, sessions and crashes. If you join with the public link, it does not show your name or email address. Feedback and screenshots you send through TestFlight go to the developer too. If you type your email address into a piece of feedback, the developer sees it with that feedback. See the TestFlight Terms of Service and Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Google Play. Google Play's help says that some data on your use of a test app is collected and shared with the developer, such as device information and app usage. It also says that only the developer can see the feedback you write about a test app. See Try new Android apps before their official release and Google's Privacy Policy.
  • The Google Group. To test on Android, you first join the Google Group cs-326-oslingo-testers. The group's owner, the course instructor, can see the email address of the Google account you join with. It is used only to give you access to the test.

The developer of OSlingo is the course instructor. OSlingo itself sends nothing to Apple, to Google or to the instructor.

Course staff

OSlingo reports nothing to the course, from the phone app or from the web. The instructor and the teaching assistants cannot see your progress or your answers.

Children

OSlingo is made for university students in CS 326. It is not directed to children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.
